# Source and writing policy

## Source order

Use the most specific current source available:

1. Primary vacancy and application portal.
2. Employer or institution career guidance.
3. Government employment service, official EU service, or national public career service relevant to the target market.
4. University career service or another clearly identified authority when the sources above do not answer the question.
5. Secondary advice only as a labeled fallback.

Search snippets are leads, not sufficient evidence. Open the source page and record the access date. Recheck time-sensitive details such as deadlines and required attachments on the primary vacancy page.

Useful starting authorities include:

- Bundesagentur für Arbeit: <https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/bildung/bewerbung/anschreiben>
- Europass cover-letter guidance: <https://europass.europa.eu/en/create-europass-cover-letter>
- UK National Careers Service: <https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/covering-letter>

These are starting points, not universal rules. Research the relevant market each time.

## Evidence map

For each important requirement, capture:

- the requirement and its source;
- the candidate evidence and its private source;
- whether the connection is direct, transferable, or unsupported;
- the proposed use in the letter.

Do not draft an unsupported connection. Ask the user or omit it.

## Draft structure

Adapt structure to the vacancy and local convention. A common concise structure is:

- why this role and organization;
- two or three evidence-backed matches;
- what the candidate can contribute;
- a direct professional close.

Avoid clichés, exaggerated praise, copied organization slogans, vacancy keyword lists, and claims the candidate could not explain in an interview.
